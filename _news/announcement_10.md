I'm delighted to share that I will give a talk titled ["The Recovering Hidden Histories project: Digital Methods to unlock the history of slavery in Colonial Paraguay"](https://www.history.ac.uk/news-events/events/recovering-hidden-histories-project-digital-methods-unlock-history-slavery-colonial-paraguay) at the Latin American History Seminar of the Institute of Historical Research (IHR), Senate House, London, on Tuesday, November 17th, from 17:30 to 19:00 (UK time). I will present the first results of our HAVI Schmidt Sciences project, which applies Handwritten Text Recognition and related computational methods to Paraguay's colonial archives. I will also introduce a database under construction that already tracks more than 20,000 enslaved or coerced individuals from 1540 to 1828 by cross-referencing national and religious archives. Following individual trajectories across the whole colonial period, I will show that slavery and coerced labour were the backbone of the colony. I will close by reflecting on how models trained on colonial script inherit colonial categories, and how our choices of ground truth, metadata, and search shape what future researchers will be able to find.
