I'm delighted to share that I will give a talk titled "An Anticolonial Vision from a Defiant Guaraní Woman Who Stood in Solidarity in Colonial Paraguay: Juliana and Her 1541 Criminal Trial" at the [VI International Congress of Indigenous Worlds (COIMI) – Europe](https://coimi.net/en/thecongress/presentation) in Liverpool next Thursday, November 12th, at 11:45 (UK time). I will speak at the World Museum in the session "Indigenous Histories & Cosmologies: Transgressing Global Designs," chaired by Juciene Ricarte Cardoso Tarairiú (UFCG). Drawing on the criminal proceedings held in the National Archive of Asunción, I will follow Juliana, an enslaved Guaraní woman who poisoned her Spanish enslaver, Nuño de Cabrera, in early colonial Asunción. Released after her first trial, she told the other Indigenous women serving the Spaniards what she had done, and was re-arrested and executed under Governor Álvar Núñez Cabeza de Vaca. I will argue that her words reveal an anticolonial political project and a practice of solidarity among enslaved Guaraní women in the first years of the colony.
