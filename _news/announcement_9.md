Thrilled to announce that next week I will be presenting at the VI International Congress of Indigenous Worlds (COIMI) – Europe, held in Liverpool on 12–13 November 2026. The congress takes place at the World Museum and the University of Liverpool.

On Thursday 12 November at 11.45am, at the World Museum, I will speak in the session “Indigenous Histories & Cosmologies: Transgressing Global Designs.” The session is chaired by Juciene Ricarte Cardoso Tarairiú (UFCG), and I will share the panel with André Roberto de Arruda Machado (UNIFESP) and Kasikeiani Ronalda ‘Kaiko T’ Pairman (Yamaye Guani Taino). My paper is titled:

“An Anticolonial Vision from a Defiant Guaraní Woman Who Stood in Solidarity in Colonial Paraguay: Juliana and Her 1541 Criminal Trial”
(Una propuesta anticolonial de una mujer guaraní combativa y solidaria en el Paraguay colonial: Juliana y su proceso criminal de 1541)

The paper draws on the criminal proceedings held in the Archivo Nacional de Asunción. It follows Juliana, an enslaved Guaraní woman who poisoned her Spanish enslaver, Nuño de Cabrera, in early colonial Asunción. She was released after her first trial and told the other Indigenous women serving the Spaniards what she had done. She was then re-arrested and executed under Governor Álvar Núñez Cabeza de Vaca. Her words are a rare trace of anticolonial political consciousness and of solidarity among enslaved Guaraní women in the first years of the colony.

After the VI COIMI in Salvador, Bahia, last February, I’m delighted to continue these conversations in Europe. COIMI brings together Indigenous and non-Indigenous researchers, museum professionals and knowledge-holders to open new decolonial paths for Indigenous histories. The two-day programme also includes roundtables on Indigenous knowledge and education and on Indigenising museums, as well as a closing workshop on reparative actions with and for Indigenous peoples.

If you’re in Liverpool next week, come and join us!
